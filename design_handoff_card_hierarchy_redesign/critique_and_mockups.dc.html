2. Critique

1 — one surface, used everywhere

.pixel-box is the app's only surface: 3px ink border, flat offset shadow, card-white fill. It's used for the calendar's day panel entries, every list card, the map's legend, the map's candidate/active card, and the sheets. That's correct as a base unit — but nothing ever sits below it or above it. A collapsed list row gets the exact same border-and-shadow treatment as the one card you're actively editing. The device bezel (6px shadow) is the only thing heavier than a card (4px), so every screen has exactly two volumes: "bezel" and "everything else." That's the real cause of "nothing has hierarchy" — it's not a spacing problem, it's a missing middle tier.

2 — the action row doesn't know which state it's in

Look at what DateCard actually renders in idle mode: edit, plus up to two of (we went / call it off / back on / how was it? / undo), plus delete, pushed right — three or four buttons of identical weight bar one pink fill. On a 375px card that's a wrapped row of buttons the instant a status label runs long. The primary action (rate it, mark it done) already gets pixel-btn-primary — the fix isn't inventing a new visual language, it's stopping there and demoting everything else, every time.

3 — empty states are inconsistent, not just plain

Three of the four empty states already carry a PixelHeart glyph and a voice ("Nothing yet. Add somewhere you both want to go."). The fourth — DayPanel's "Nothing here yet." for a day with no plans — drops both. It's not that empty states need character added; one of them is missing character the others already have.

4 — lavender and aqua carry real meaning, it's just contextual

Lavender means "idea" (unpinned pin colour, outline heart, linked-day highlight); aqua means "done" (pin colour, cold-weather badge, the verdict divider). That's a real, consistent system — it just only surfaces on a map pin or a rating divider, never in the chrome. The list, which has four sections mapped to exactly these same statuses, uses none of it: "someday" gets a plain grey count, "been there" gets nothing at all. The system exists; it stops one layer short of the screen where it would do the most work.

5 — desktop is three bordered boxes, not one console

The three panes are separated by a 3px ink border and nothing else — no shared header, no visual signal that they're one device rather than three adjacent windows. The ideas column is also fixed at 16rem (256px), narrower than a single mobile card (375px minus padding), so the same card that breathes on a phone gets squeezed on the screen with the most room.

6 — nothing on screen is about the two of you

Checked every screen: the most personal text in the app is the raw sign-in email at the bottom of the list. There's no name, no couple identity, no sense of history (how many dates, how long you've been doing this) anywhere in the primary views. Agree this is the weakest point of the six — it's a real gap, not a matter of taste.

3. Proposed changes

add a middle surface tier: flat rows vs. raised cards

Collapsed list rows drop the border and shadow entirely — just the row content on the paper background, separated by a 1px hairline at 20% ink. The full pixel-box treatment (3px border, 4px shadow) is reserved for whichever single card is expanded or active. Calendar day cells and the map's own background stay flat paper too, so the raised cards are the only things that visually "sit on top" of a screen. This is a token change, not a per-screen one: two new classes, .row-flat and .row-active, sitting between .pixel-box and plain text.

collapse the action row to one button + an overflow sheet

Idle state shows exactly one pixel-btn-primary action (whichever is contextually primary — "we went," "how was it?," or nothing if there's truly nothing to do) plus a single 44px "⋯" button. Edit / call it off / undo / delete move into the sheet component that already exists for EditSheet — no new UI pattern, just a second use of one you've already built. Delete stays behind a confirm step exactly as today.

give every empty state the same glyph and voice

Give DayPanel's empty state the outline PixelHeart the other three already use, and let its line match their tone: "Nothing here yet — pick a day with something on it, or add one." One-line fix, makes all four consistent.

extend the pin colours into the list's section headers

Each agenda section heading gets a small PixelHeart in its status colour instead of a plain count: lavender outline heart for "someday" (idea), aqua filled heart for "been there" (done), the existing hot badge stays on "next up" (planned). No new colour meaning invented — this is the map's own legend, reused as the list's legend, so the two views teach each other instead of one being pink-only and the other being the "real" system.

a shared strip above the three desktop panes

A slim header — nameplate + a lifetime counter ("14 dates · 3 years") — runs above all three panes so they read as one console with a shared top edge, the way the mobile tab bar already unifies the phone. Ideas pane widens from 16rem to roughly 20rem so a card isn't narrower there than it is on a 375px phone.

a nameplate, not an email address

Add an editable device nameplate ("Mina & Jules") in Pixelify Sans, shown on the bezel or the desktop strip above, and a small lifetime stat near sign-out. Sign-out keeps the email — it's still useful for confirming which account — but it stops being the only personal string in the app.

No hard constraint is broken by any of this: hot pink stays fills/borders-only (list headings above use it only as a badge fill, never as text colour — text stays deep pink or ink); every tap target below is 44px+; inputs are untouched; the map keeps Google's colours; category icons stay emoji. The overflow sheet is the one new-looking pattern, and it isn't new — it's EditSheet's existing sheet, reused.
